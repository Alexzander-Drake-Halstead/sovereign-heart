Project Context

The Sovereign Heart $p\text{-}^{11}\text{B}$ solid-state fusion reactor proxy prototype.

Current fusion energy research relies heavily on multi-billion-dollar tokamak infrastructure and steam-cycle conversion, creating severe economic and scaling bottlenecks. The Sovereign Heart shifts the focus to a localized, solid-state approach utilizing aneutronic proton-boron ($p\text{-}^{11}\text{B}$) fusion. By combining an $sp^3$ diamond matrix core with solid-state bismuth telluride ($\text{Bi}_2\text{Te}_3$) direct energy conversion, this architecture bypasses steam turbines and thermal generators entirely. A 192 kg benchtop proxy is already constructed; this campaign funds the final component integration and verification stages.

Significance

Unlike traditional deuterium-tritium reactions, proton-boron ($p\text{-}^{11}\text{B}$) fusion yields no primary neutron radiation, eliminating the need for massive concrete shielding and allowing for extremely compact footprints. Demonstrating continuous 66 kW output via solid-state direct energy conversion proves that baseload power can be generated in a modular, mobile format without moving parts, water loops, or reliance on centralized grid infrastructure.
